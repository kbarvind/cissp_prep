# Types of Evidence in Investigations (CISSP - Domain 7)

In CISSP Domain 7 (Security Operations), understanding the different types of evidence used in investigations is critical for forensics, compliance, and legal proceedings.

---

## 🔹 1. Real Evidence (Physical Evidence)
- **Definition**: Tangible objects relevant to the case.
- **Examples**: 
  - Damaged hardware (e.g., a hard drive with malware)
  - Printed documents
  - Physical access logs or key cards

---

## 🔹 2. Documentary Evidence
- **Definition**: Written or recorded materials.
- **Examples**:
  - System logs
  - Emails
  - Configuration files
  - Security policies and procedures

---

## 🔹 3. Testimonial Evidence
- **Definition**: Oral or written statements made by witnesses.
- **Examples**:
  - Interviews or depositions
  - Statements from employees or investigators

---

## 🔹 4. Demonstrative Evidence
- **Definition**: Evidence used to clarify or explain other evidence.
- **Examples**:
  - Charts or diagrams of network activity
  - Attack timelines
  - Reconstructed attack flows

---

## 🔹 5. Direct Evidence
- **Definition**: Evidence that directly proves a fact.
- **Examples**:
  - Video surveillance showing unauthorized access
  - A log file showing a specific IP address performing an attack

---

## 🔹 6. Circumstantial Evidence (Indirect Evidence)
- **Definition**: Evidence that suggests a fact by implication or inference.
- **Examples**:
  - Patterns of suspicious behavior
  - Logs showing only one user was logged in during a breach

---

## 🔹 7. Best Evidence
- **Definition**: The most reliable form of evidence available.
- **Examples**:
  - Original logs or files
  - Forensic images from original drives

---

## 🔹 8. Secondary Evidence
- **Definition**: Copies or reproductions used when the original is unavailable.
- **Examples**:
  - Printouts of logs
  - Witness descriptions of a screen

---

## 🔹 9. Hearsay Evidence
- **Definition**: Second-hand information, generally inadmissible in court.
- **Examples**:
  - “Someone told me that they saw him breach the system.”

---

## 🔹 10. Digital Evidence
- **Definition**: Information stored or transmitted in digital form.
- **Examples**:
  - Logs, emails, databases
  - PCAP files
  - Browser history

---

## 🔹 11. Corroborative Evidence
- **Definition**: Supports or confirms other evidence.
- **Examples**:
  - Multiple logs showing the same activity
  - Testimony that aligns with forensic data

---

## 🧩 Additional Key Concepts

- **Chain of Custody**: Maintains documentation of evidence handling.
- **Integrity of Evidence**: Protected via hashing (e.g., SHA-256).
- **Admissibility**: Evidence must be relevant, authentic, and legally obtained.


---

The hearsay rule says that a witness cannot testify about what someone else told them, except under very specific exceptions. The courts have applied the hearsay rule to include the concept that attorneys may not introduce logs into evidence unless they are authenticated by the system administrator.
